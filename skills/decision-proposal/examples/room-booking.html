Room booking — proposal

People book a meeting room for a block of time, from a day view of every room on a floor or by asking for "a room for 6 at 2 pm". The database refuses a double booking, so two people clicking at once can't both win. A booking that nobody checks in to within 10 minutes of its start is released, so rooms booked "just in case" go back to the people who need them. Bookings can repeat weekly, a few rooms need approval, and Facilities can take a room out of service without stranding the people who had it. It is not a calendar: it sends calendar invites and keeps them in step, but your calendar stays where it is. Desk booking is not part of this.

How it works, and what was decided

The model

  1. The database refuses a double booking. An exclusion constraint on room and time range, for bookings that aren't cancelled or released. The screen's "Free" is a hint; the insert is the check, and a clash comes back as frame 3a.
  2. Times are instants, shown in the room's zone. A weekly 14:00 stays 14:00 in the room across a clock change, so a series is stored as a rule plus its dates, not 24 copies of one instant.
  3. Release keeps rooms honest. A booking is a promise to turn up. No check-in by +10 min frees the room and tells the organiser; it never deletes the booking's history.
  4. It is not a calendar. We send invites and keep them in step. We never read or keep what people write in them.
  5. Three permissions: book, approve (per room), manage (Facilities). No one needs a new role to book a room.

Decisions (Sam Rivera, Workplace lead, 3 Oct 2026)

  1. Auto-release: yes, at start + 10 minutes, changeable per building and room. As recommended.
  2. How long a repeat can run: 6 months, then the organiser is asked to renew. As recommended.
  3. Clashes in a repeat: book the free dates and offer a room for each clash (frame 3b). As recommended.
  4. A big room for a small group: warn ("Bigger than you need"), don't block. As recommended.
  5. Approval rooms: the request holds the slot; unanswered by the start = declined. Changed: recommended was "approved if unanswered"; Sam wants the safer default.
  6. Titles: shown to everyone by default. Changed: a Private switch on the booking hides title and organiser (frame 1b, table 7).
  7. Out of service: move bookings to a similar room where one is free, cancel the rest, tell every organiser. New: frame 3d.
  8. Longest booking: 4 hours; longer needs approval. As recommended.
  9. Buffer between bookings: unanswered, taken as recommended (none; people can end early).

New questions from the revision (recommendations in italics)

  1. N1 · Three no-shows in a row on a repeat? Releasing each one quietly hides that the series is dead. On the third, email the organiser: keep the series, or cancel the rest. Never cancel it automatically.
  2. N2 · Who owns a booking Facilities moved? The original organiser, still. The move is logged with who did it, and the organiser can move it back if the old room frees up.
  3. N3 · Check in without a tablet? Rooms without one would never release. Yes, from the booking link or calendar invite, from 10 minutes before to 10 minutes after the start, and only for people on the booking.

Build (after sign-off)

  • Slice 1: bookings with the exclusion constraint, the day view, the booking dialog, private, cancel and end early, the calendar invite.
  • Slice 2: Find me a room, repeating bookings with the clash dialog.
  • Slice 3: room tablets, check-in (tablet and link), auto-release and its email, Book now.
  • Slice 4: approval rooms, out of service with move/cancel.
  • Every slice: a database test that two concurrent inserts can't both succeed, a time-zone test across a clock change, and an end-to-end booking test. Slice 3 gets a security review (a shared device in a corridor).
